Understanding FIRE

Financial Independence, Retire Early (FIRE) is a movement focused on smart saving and investing, allowing you to buy back your time and choose whether, when, and how you want to work.

What is FIRE?

At its core, FIRE is about freedom. By increasing the gap between what you earn and what you spend, you save a significant portion of your income and invest it in stable assets (like low-cost index funds).

Once your investment portfolio grows large enough that you can safely live off the returns indefinitely without ever touching the principal, you have achieved Financial Independence.

How It Works: Sarah's Example

Sarah's Profile: Age 30. She earns $90,000 post-tax, spends $40,000 annually on her lifestyle, and saves the remaining $50,000.

Her Goal: Sarah wants to achieve financial freedom. To find her target FIRE Number, she simply divides her annual expenses ($40,000) by her Safe Withdrawal Rate (4%, or 0.04):

$40,000 ÷ 0.04 = $1,000,000

Starting with $100,000 in savings and contributing $50,000 each year at an 8% return, Sarah will reach her target of $1,000,000 in 12 years at age 42.

Choose Your FIRE Path

Traditional FIRE

Aiming to retire with a nest egg that matches your current standard of living. Usually assumes an annual retirement spend of $40,000 to $80,000 based on standard expenses.

Lean FIRE

A minimalist approach. Aiming for an annual spend of $40,000 or less. Requires strict budgeting and a highly optimized, low-cost lifestyle.

Fat FIRE

An abundant approach. Aiming for an annual spend of $100,000+ to maintain a luxurious lifestyle, travel extensively, or live in high-cost-of-living metropolitan areas.

Barista FIRE

Achieving partial independence. You save enough to cover core survival, then quit your high-stress job to work part-time or in a passion field for side income and healthcare benefits.

Expat FIRE

Geographic arbitrage. Retiring early by relocating to a country with a much lower cost of living, allowing your retirement funds to stretch significantly further.
